# P3.1 design gate — reconciliation outline

**Status:** required planning artifact; not an approved implementation spec.

This outline converts the reconciled research into the decisions, models, and
tests required before P3.3-P3.11 implementation. It deliberately does not add
systems merely because a reference game has them.

## Canonical first-run critical path

The first 30 minutes have one strict, honest path. Each step teaches a system
that is immediately usable and creates the condition for the next one:

1. Stabilize the Command Module and read a visible resource need.
2. Hire and assign the first crew member.
3. Build and staff the first producer; show its output changing.
4. Mine a grade-1 asteroid and receive the vessel's return.
5. Use the Trading Post to resolve a visible resource mismatch.
6. Upgrade the Command Module as a qualitative capability unlock.
7. Follow a relay signal on the first expedition; it guarantees an NPC
   relationship and a Research Lab blueprint.
8. Build the Research Lab.
9. Complete a first research entry from Growth, Economy, or Battle.
10. Complete a second expedition, demonstrating a normal weighted outcome and
    target-specific survey progress.
11. Replace the strict chain with concurrent, explained milestones.

The first expedition is a truthful sample of the continuing loop: target,
visible time/fuel/risk proposition, timed travel, outcome, and a new decision.
Derelict stations, moons/outposts, anomalies, and distress signals enter the
repeatable pool afterwards.

## Confirmed implementation constraints

Objective state is authoritative, revisioned snapshot data. The server advances
it as a side effect of a verified action or scheduler settlement, emits an
objective push, and verifies a separate reward-claim action. After the relay
fragment returns, the first Research Lab entry completes, and a second
expedition returns, the snapshot changes from one current objective to a
concurrent optional-milestone set.

Every repeatable expedition settlement also increments revisioned,
target-specific survey progress, regardless of the rolled outcome. Crossing its
bounded threshold yields a guaranteed meaningful milestone. This progress is
bad-luck protection, not a new currency, and it coexists with diminishing
returns for repeatedly visiting the same target.

## Required model before numeric tuning

Create one authoritative design model for normal and accelerated simulation. It
must state, for each of the first 30 minutes, three hours, several days, and the
provisional longer campaign:

- resource and fuel sources, sinks, caps, expected balances, and recovery path;
- construction, research, mining, expedition, repair, and combat wait windows;
- objective/reward injections and the available active alternative during each
  wait;
- expedition target fatigue, bounded discovery progress, and bad-luck
  protection;
- recurring solo-challenge and arena reward curves; and
- crew-cost and vessel-capacity curves.

Do not select expedition percentages, a slot formula, capacity scaling, or arena
material rewards until this model shows that no all-day loop dominates a normal
return cadence.

## Combat and arena proof

P3.10 must specify the player's actual decisions: loadout, vessel role,
readiness information, target condition, counter/route/preparation choice, and
the combat report that tells a losing player what to change. The game displays
both a named readiness tier and a numeric Power value, each backed by the same
clear breakdown; neither can substitute for those explanations.

Combat dispatch occupies its selected fitted vessel. Other ship-side work and
other available vessels continue, so the test is an opportunity cost rather than
a whole-game pause. P3.1 must state the early-vessel availability needed to make
that true.

The local arena is an explicit P3.10-adjacent proof, not a proxy for online PvP:

- a profile opt-in publishes an immutable, validated defense snapshot;
- a challenge mutates only attacker/ranking/reward state, never defender state;
- no looting, shared-world contention, or live opponent is implied;
- curated NPC/ghost defenses make the mode usable before several local profiles
  exist; and
- victories have a tangible, capped reward: deterministic drone/troop upgrade
  components rather than base resources, random pulls, or a captain-collection
  system; losses award no material consolation; and
- its reward curve cannot become the best resource-income loop.

## Content and evaluation plan

Before declaring the campaign duration plausible, budget the authored/data
content needed per radial band: objectives, research decisions, vessel choices,
expedition target archetypes, NPC behaviors, hazards, and capital clues. A
multi-week claim needs a content cadence, not only larger costs.

P3.19 observes and records at least:

- time to first meaningful ship/map choice and first expedition;
- first confusing prerequisite and time spent with no useful action;
- objective completion/claim sequence and point where guided content ends;
- route, research, and vessel-choice diversity;
- failure cause, recovery time, and whether the report taught a next action;
- offline-return outcome and time to the next decision; and
- outcomes separately for first-session and multi-hour returning players.

An abrupt post-onboarding wait/prerequisite wall is a P0 rebalance failure.

## Resolved cross-track decisions

- Keep trade and the Command Module capability upgrade before the first relay
  expedition.
- Command Module levels mechanically unlock usable room bands/slots in Phase 3,
  with visible prerequisite explanations; no individual tile-clearing loop is
  required.
- Treat repair as a primary-sink experiment, subject to P3.1 simulation and
  P3.19 playtest results rather than assumed balance.

## Decisions reserved for product review

1. Exact research respec policy and its normal-play cost.
2. P3.11 reset, carry-over, and ascension-tree contract, after a dedicated
   finite-prestige/New Game+ reference pass.
