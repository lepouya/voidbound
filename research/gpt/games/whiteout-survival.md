# Whiteout Survival — evidence notes

## Product frame

Whiteout Survival presents a glacial-apocalypse settlement led around a furnace:
players manage people and resources, recruit heroes, develop technology, collect
resources on the ice field, and fight with/against other chiefs [S01] [S02]. Its
current store page deliberately frames the accessible layer as “rebuild,” idle
rewards, quick activities, hero combinations, and alliance success [S02].

## Core loop

1. Upgrade the furnace/headquarters and the buildings needed to unlock the next
   furnace level.
2. Gather and passively earn resources; manage city output and troop capacity.
3. Recruit/upgrade heroes and troops; use them for PvE, map activity, and
   combat.
4. Join an alliance for help, healing, rewards, and coordinated progression.
5. Save speedups/materials for scored events and state-versus-state cycles.

The furnace is a highly legible central progression object: its upgrade is both
an immediate visual goal and the gate to new capabilities. The risk is that it
can collapse strategy into headquarters rushing, where supporting buildings only
exist to satisfy prerequisites.

## Supporting engagement systems

- Survivor/city presentation and warmth theme; resource nodes and monsters on
  the world map [S01] [S02].
- Hero collection/skills and troop composition [S01] [S02].
- Alliance help, rallies, healing, shared rewards, and group events [S02].
- Repeated event calendar, rankings, castle battles, cross-state competition,
  and later Fire Crystal progression, based on player reports [S03].
- Quick side activities and idle collection are explicitly positioned as
  short-session relief [S02].

## Progression experience (research hypothesis)

| Stage | What the player experiences                                                       | What seems to work                                       | Risk to test                                                                                                                           |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Start | Furnace as survival icon, rapid town rebuilding, survivors, simple resource jobs. | Strong theme/action alignment and an obvious next goal.  | Ensure the early survival premise remains mechanically relevant after onboarding.                                                      |
| Mid   | Furnace/building requirements, heroes/troops, alliance routines, event timing.    | The player sees a growing town and gains group identity. | Saving resources for calendar scoring can make ordinary play feel suboptimal.                                                          |
| End   | Fire Crystal levels and recurring alliance, state, and cross-state events.        | High-stakes social spectacle for invested players.       | A Reddit thread describes no finite ending, dominance, repeated events, and painful PvP loss; treat this as community sentiment [S03]. |

## Community feedback to preserve

The most useful contrast is not whether Whiteout is “good” or “bad,” but why
players remain: cooperation, shared events, and a visually comprehensible city
can be compelling. The frequently reported cost is a schedule-bound, unequal
long game: community discussions describe spender advantage, routine fatigue,
and a loop with no authored conclusion [S03]; the current store reviews also
complain about purchase prompts and poor respect for time [S02].

## Applicability to Voidbound

**Borrow:** one central, visibly evolving Command Module; a readable settlement
state; returns that deliver a meaningful collected result; short optional side
activities; and a map goal beyond raw production.

**Avoid in Phase 3:** an endless headquarters treadmill, stockpiling solely to
win external scoring windows, irreversible troop loss, and making a group a
requirement for core advancement. Voidbound’s Command Module should unlock
qualitatively different choices (research, expeditions, regions), not merely a
higher number.
