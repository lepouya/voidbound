# Dark War: Survival — evidence notes

## Product frame

Florere Game presents Dark War as a zombie-apocalypse shelter-survival game:
survivors establish a shelter and rebuild civilization [S04]. Community guidance
and discussions show that the durable game is a server-based 4X loop of base
power, heroes/troops, alliances, rallies, timed events, and seasonal territory
competition—not only the survival scene shown in acquisition material [S05]
[S06].

## Core loop

1. Improve the shelter and its production/training capability.
2. Assign or develop survivors/heroes and concentrate scarce upgrade materials
   into a main fighting force.
3. Gather/fight on the world map, join alliance rallies, and collect rewards.
4. Convert resources, speedups, event rewards, and gear into more power.
5. Use that power in alliance/server events; repeat against higher progression
   thresholds.

The survivor-job premise is attractive because it makes a settlement feel
inhabited. In practice, community guidance strongly emphasizes concentration of
hero investment and alliance activity as the compounding progression choices
[S05]. That is a useful warning: visible worker assignment can become cosmetic
if most meaningful decisions converge on one optimal combat build.

## Supporting engagement systems

- Hero collection, factions, rarity, gear, and march composition [S05].
- Alliance help reduces construction time; rallies and seasonal resources make
  coordinated membership materially valuable [S05].
- Beginner event structure: community documentation describes the five-day
  Survivor Trials as an early teaching/reward ladder [S07].
- Territory, server diplomacy, NAP arrangements, seasonal events, rankings, and
  social coordination [S05] [S06].

## Progression experience (research hypothesis)

| Stage | What the player experiences                                                            | What seems to work                                                                      | Risk to test                                                                                                                                          |
| ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Start | Shelter fantasy, rapid unlocks, survivor/hero introductions, a newcomer event.         | Frequent new capabilities and a clear “repair civilization” theme.                      | The advertised survival fantasy may imply a different game from the live 4X loop [S04] [S06].                                                         |
| Mid   | Power optimization, active alliance membership, rallies, gear, and season preparation. | Social belonging and visible collective wins; a strong alliance removes friction [S05]. | Progress becomes calendar/efficiency dependent and opaque to a casual newcomer.                                                                       |
| End   | Season/territory competition, dominant alliances, high-end hero and gear growth.       | Long-lived teams have shared coordination goals.                                        | Community reports describe power concentration, spending pressure, and toxicity; this is perception evidence, not a verified universal outcome [S06]. |

## Community feedback to preserve

Positive: people describe alliance friends and coordination as a reason to stay
[S06]. Negative: discussions report a gap between ads and the actual loop,
spending pressure, hostility, weak moderation, and the ability of very strong
players to dominate a server [S04] [S06]. These reports lead to a Phase 3 rule:
Voidbound must offer a satisfying, self-contained solo reason to return that
does not depend on social pressure, a dominant player, or purchasing recovery.

## Applicability to Voidbound

**Borrow:** a concrete first-week objective ladder; workers/crew whose role is
legible; expeditions/rallies as visible departures and returns; seasonal-map
ambition as an eventual online layer.

**Avoid in Phase 3:** leaderboard-driven optimization, time-zone coordination,
PvP loss, alliance membership as a mandatory power multiplier, and unbounded
hero/gear acquisition. A local alpha should make the player’s crew, vessel, and
module choices matter without needing a meta guide.
