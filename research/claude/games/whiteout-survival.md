# Whiteout Survival

## 1. Overview

- **Genre:** 4X-lite base-building strategy / survival-city-builder, marketed
  with heavy "SLG" (simulation/strategy) DNA — visually and structurally a
  spiritual descendant of _Frostpunk_, mechanically a cousin of _Rise of
  Kingdoms_ / _Last War: Survival_ / _State of Survival_.
- **Developer/Publisher:** Century Games Pte. Ltd. (Singapore-headquartered
  mobile publisher).
- **Release date:** Sources disagree slightly on the exact date — one stats
  aggregator (Udonis) lists a soft-launch date of December 2, 2022, while the
  widely-cited global launch date is **February 12, 2023** (iOS/Android
  worldwide). China launched separately in May 2024.
- **Platforms:** iOS and Android (free-to-play, no PC/console release found in
  research).
- **Scale (as of research date, August 2026):**
  - Lifetime player spending has passed **$3 billion** (surpassed in under 2.5
    years post-launch per PocketGamer.biz/Sensor Tower).
  - All-time downloads estimated around **188 million** (Udonis).
  - 2023 revenue ~$271M → 2024 ~$1.02B → 2025
    ~$1.49B (peak year); January 2026 alone brought in ~$101.6M, described as
    "essentially flat month-on-month" versus prior months.
  - Peak monthly revenue was **$205.9M in March 2025** (126% YoY growth at that
    point).
  - DAU consistently cited above 2-3 million; MAU in the 9-14 million range
    depending on source/month; DAU/MAU ratio around 24%, called out as unusually
    "sticky" for the genre.
  - Revenue per download cited around **$17.90-$18** — many times a typical F2P
    game, reflecting a whale-heavy monetization base.
  - Geographic revenue mix (per PocketGamer/Sensor Tower): US ~28%
    ($870.3M), China ~17% ($531.2M, since May 2024 launch), South Korea ~13-36%
    depending on source/period, Japan also called out as a strong market.
  - It was reported as the **third-fastest 2023-cohort game to reach $1
    billion** in revenue, and has held a top-5 grossing mobile-game position
    through most of 2025-2026.
- **Monetization model:** Free-to-play, in-app-purchase only — reviewers note it
  runs **no ads** in the ad-supported sense, monetizing entirely through IAP:
  gem/premium-currency packs, a permanent-effect VIP subscription/level system,
  multiple concurrent "battle pass"-style tracks, gacha-style hero shard packs,
  speed-up timers, and limited-time bundles. Described by one analysis as a
  "whale-driven economy" — daily active ad-creative count for user acquisition
  is estimated around ~2,000 concurrent creatives, indicating an aggressive,
  constantly-rotating performance-marketing engine (UA style borrowed from
  casual/hypercasual playbooks rather than traditional midcore marketing).

## 2. Core gameplay loop

A single session braids three concurrent layers, per multiple sources (App Store
copy + independent review):

1. **City/survival management:** Keep the Furnace (the central hub building) fed
   and running — if it goes out, citizens die. Assign survivors to jobs (hunter,
   cook, woodcutter, miner, etc.), manage worker happiness/meal quality (which
   affects productivity), and queue building upgrades.
2. **Idle hero dispatch:** Send heroes out on one-tap "Idle & Earn"
   expeditions/gathering runs, then return later to collect accumulated
   resources — explicitly designed as a low-friction, stress-free idle loop
   ("Send your heroes out with one tap, then come back to a pile of resources").
3. **Tap-and-play mini-activities:** Short, on-demand diversions like ice
   fishing and treasure-hunting ("dig up buried treasure beneath the snow") that
   fill quick sessions.

Layered on top: periodic PvE combat (map monsters/beasts, "Defeat Nearby
Beasts"), resource-node gathering marches sent out into the open world, and
alliance-coordinated PvP/rally events on a rotating weekly/monthly schedule (see
§5). The official app-store pitch frames five pillars: **Rebuild & Restore**
(base building), **Idle & Earn** (passive hero income), **Tap & Play**
(mini-games), **Plan & Pair** (hero team-building), **Unite & Triumph**
(alliance play).

## 3. Base building & progression systems

- **Furnace = city level.** The Furnace is the central gating building; its
  level caps the max level of every other building (Heroes Hall, Infirmary,
  Embassy, etc.). Standard progression runs Furnace level 1 through **30**,
  after which eligible/mature servers unlock a post-cap "**Fire Crystal**" era,
  FC1 through FC10, extending development further.
- **Key building-unlock breakpoints** cited by guides: Furnace 4 unlocks Shelter
  3/Clinic/Explorer's Cabin/Hero Hall; Furnace 8 unlocks
  Embassy/Arena/Monument/Marksman Camp/Infirmary; Furnace 9 unlocks
  Storehouse/Research Center/Lancer Camp; Furnace 10 unlocks the Command Center;
  Furnace 15 unlocks Hero Gear (via the Workshop); Furnace 16 is the minimum to
  join the monthly State-vs-State PvP event; Furnace 22 unlocks **Chief Gear**
  (a "massive power spike" most guides recommend rushing toward); Furnace 25
  unlocks Chief Gear Charms; Furnace 30 unlocks Fire Crystal progression.
- **Heroes.** Collected via gacha (gems/"Frost Stars" premium currency),
  organized in three classes — **Infantry** (frontline/defensive), **Lancer**
  (flexible/utility/rally support), **Marksman** (ranged damage). Squads are
  five-hero teams. Heroes level to a max of 80; leveling increases
  Attack/Defense/HP and (in Expedition mode) troop-lead capacity. A notable QoL
  system, the **Drill Camp**, lets players sync all heroes' levels to their
  top-five hero, reducing per-hero grind — one reviewer called this "drastically
  reducing the grind."
- **Hero generations / power creep.** New hero generations release roughly
  **every ~80 days** of server age, explicitly designed as a recurring
  power-creep cycle rather than sidegrades — described by community tier-list
  writers as leaving prior generations "in the dust." By mid-2026 the game was
  already at Generation 16-17 on mature servers. Community guidance is to "focus
  on one key upgrade per gen and save the rest" rather than chase every release.
- **Gear systems:** Two parallel gear tracks — **Hero Gear** (per-hero, three
  pieces: head/body/feet, unlocked Furnace 15/Workshop) and **Chief Gear**
  (account-wide, six main pieces tied to troop types — Infantry/Lancer/Marksman
  — boosting attack/defense, unlocked Furnace 22, with Charms added at Furnace
  25).
- **Research (tech tree).** The Research Center has three parallel trees:
  **Growth** (build/research speed, troop training/deployment capacity),
  **Economy** (resource production rates, gathering speed), and
  **Battle/Military** (troop attack/defense/lethality by type, survival/assault
  technique bonuses, equipment stat bonuses, formation bonuses). Costs scale
  through Wood/Meat/Coal/Iron and eventually Steel.
- **Power/CP score:** Referred to as "Squad Power," derived from a formula
  combining Hero Power and Troop Level (exact formula not published by the
  developer in sources found); aggregated account power is the primary
  rank/leaderboard metric referenced throughout PvP events.
- **Troop tiers:** Troops (Infantry/Lancer/Marksman) upgrade through tiers **T1
  ("Rookie") through T11 ("Helios")**, with a T12/endgame tier referenced as a
  late-game bracket marker in server-age guides.
- **Server/state age brackets:** Progression is explicitly staged by server
  calendar age, not just player effort — e.g., Tundra territory opens to
  alliances Day 14, Fertile Land Day 39, Sunfire Castle (the SvS battleground)
  Day 53, new hero generation roughly every 80 days, War Academy around Day 230.
  Community-tracked "brackets" run Furnace 30 → Fire Crystal 1-3 → FC 4-5 → FC
  6-8 → FC 9-10 → T12/endgame.

## 4. Resource economy

- **Core resources:** **Wood, Meat, Coal, and Iron** are the four primary
  production resources (Steel appears as a higher-tier research/crafting
  material). Coal is called out by guides as critical to not run out of "during
  winter storms" (a periodic weather/survival mechanic tied to the Furnace).
- **Production:** Driven by worker job assignments (hunter, cook, woodcutter,
  miner, etc.) at production buildings, further modified by worker
  happiness/morale (poor meal quality reduces morale and slows output) and by
  Economy-tree research bonuses.
- **Gathering:** Troops can also be marched out to resource nodes on the open
  map to gather Wood/Meat/Coal/Iron directly, with gathering-speed bonuses from
  research and hero skills.
- **Storage & caps:** The **Storehouse** building defines storage capacity,
  scaling from 5,000 at level 1 up to **120,000,000 at level 30**. Resources
  within the Storehouse cap cannot be plundered by other players when attacked;
  any surplus above the cap is exposed and can be looted. This creates an
  explicit "don't over-hoard without upgrading storage/using shields" strategic
  tension.
- **Shields:** Players get temporary Shields (2h/8h/24h durations) that block
  all enemy attacks and scouting; players are granted 5 free 8-hour shields on a
  7-day cooldown, used defensively (e.g., going offline while holding a large
  stockpile).
- **Spend sinks:** Building/research upgrades, troop training, hero
  leveling/promotion, gacha pulls for heroes and shards, Chief/Hero Gear
  crafting and upgrading (with dedicated material types), Chief Orders
  (city-wide temporary buffs unlocked via the Chief's House at Furnace 6),
  speed-up items for timers, and the recurring event economy (event-specific
  currencies like Explosive Arrowheads for Bear Hunt, Hope points for Vision of
  Dawn, etc.).

## 5. Combat systems

- **PvE:**
  - Open-world beast/monster hunting ("Defeat Nearby Beasts") as a stamina-gated
    activity.
  - **Bear Hunt** — a flagship recurring alliance PvE event: alliance leaders
    (R4/R5) build a Hunting Trap in alliance territory, members contribute
    "Explosive Arrowheads" (from beast hunts) to enhance it up to level 5 (+5%
    attack per level, +25% max), then the alliance **rallies** the "Raging Bear"
    for a 30-minute damage-race window (repeatable roughly every 47.5 hours / ~2
    days), with rewards scaled to individual damage contribution. Only the first
    four players to join a given rally can contribute an Expedition skill buff
    (based on join order and their first hero's skill).
  - **Frostdragon Tyrant** — a monthly alliance-vs-alliance
    PvE/territory-control event centered on occupying the "Frostdragon Capital"
    and controlling a "Spire," with rare "Dawn Honor" rewards.
  - **Crazy Joe** — a defense-coordination event (waves of attackers) that
    rewards Chief Gear materials and scales with state/server progression.
  - Exploration/"Expedition" mode battles using 5-hero teams (durable frontline
    tank + sustained-damage hero + support/healer is the community-standard
    formation).
- **PvP:**
  - **Rallies** are the core PvP verb: one player initiates an attack march, and
    other alliance members join troops onto that same march so the combined
    force lands as one blow — used both against PvE bosses (Bear Hunt) and
    against enemy player targets/fortresses.
  - **State vs State (SvS / "State of Power")** is described as the single
    largest monthly event: two states are matchmade and compete across four
    phases — **Matchmaking, Preparation** (5 scoring days, each with a daily
    theme: Construction, Research, Beasts, Training, Power Boost — each with its
    own leaderboard/milestones), **Battle** (players can teleport into the rival
    state and fight any unshielded player), and **Field Triage**. The state that
    loses Preparation has its "Sunfire Castle" opened for the winning state to
    attempt to capture; whoever captures it becomes "President." Minimum Furnace
    level 16 to participate.
  - **Fortress Battles** — a weekly event where each state's 12 Fortresses and 4
    Strongholds can be seized by alliances; owning territory yields passive
    alliance resource income (buildings inside alliance territory
    auto-contribute resources hourly to an alliance stockpile).
  - **Canyon Clash / Foundry Battle / Frostfire Mine** — additional
    PvP/battlefield-style events (objective control, structured alliance
    battlefields, competitive resource-collection ranking, respectively).
  - **Garrisons/traps/defense:** Players build up defensive hero/troop garrisons
    and gear specifically for defense; the Storehouse cap + Shields (above) form
    the primary anti-plunder defensive layer outside of active combat.

## 6. Alliance/social systems

- Alliances are the organizing social unit; leadership roles are **R4/R5**
  (officer/leader ranks) who set strategic plans, choose rally timing, assign
  rally leaders, and manage diplomacy/NAP (Non-Aggression Pact) rules with other
  alliances.
- **Alliance Territory:** a claimable map region around the alliance's holdings;
  resource buildings (Iron Mines, Woodmills, etc.) located inside alliance
  territory automatically contribute a portion of their output to a shared
  alliance stockpile every hour. Players get free teleports to relocate their
  city inside friendly alliance territory.
- **Diplomacy:** informal, player-negotiated — NAPs between alliances, broader
  "state politics" that guides say are important to understand for an alliance
  to thrive; no formal in-game diplomacy UI was found in sources, it's largely
  alliance-chat/Discord-driven.
- **Alliance chat** is described by an independent reviewer as where "**the true
  'game' happens**" — i.e., coordination for Fortress Battles, Alliance
  Championship, rally timing, and cross-alliance politics happens substantially
  outside the base game loop, in chat and (heavily) in third-party **Discord
  servers**.
- **Gifting/mutual aid:** Alliance members can help speed up each other's
  building/research queues (a common genre convention referenced indirectly via
  "healing faster, growing stronger... together" in the official pitch), and
  alliance-level events (Alliance Mobilization, Alliance Showdown, Alliance
  Championship) convert individual member activity into shared alliance
  rewards/standing.
- **Cross-server/cross-state structure:** the SvS event explicitly pits whole
  states/servers against each other, making the alliance the mid-tier social
  unit between individual player and full server population.

## 7. Additional/side features

(Kept separate from the core loop per the requested structure — these are
event/content-calendar layers, not the daily default loop.)

- **Mini-games:** Fishing Tournament (ice-fishing collection mini-game),
  treasure-digging ("Tap & Play" pitch line) — added, per a 2026 review,
  specifically "to break up the monotony of the 4X grind."
- **Solo scoring events:** Vision of Dawn (daily-task "Hope points" event), Hall
  of Chiefs (early-state multi-stage scoring: construction/hero
  development/training/gathering/gear), King of Icefield (larger multi-stage
  scoring competition), Lucky Wheel (hero-shard-focused gacha-adjacent event).
- **Seasonal/limited events:** Frosty Fortune, Tundra Albums, Tundra Games, and
  holiday-themed events (per event-guide roundup).
- **Chief Orders:** city-wide temporary edicts/buffs affecting productivity,
  survivors, and resource development, unlocked via the Chief's House at Furnace
  level 6 — a distinct system from Research.
- **VIP system:** a persistent VIP level/tier (funded via login streaks and/or
  purchases) referenced multiple times as gating premium shop access and
  providing account-wide bonuses; exact tier mechanics not fully detailed in
  sources found.
- **Collections/tier-list meta-game:** a large community-guide ecosystem (tier
  lists, hero investment guides, calculators — WoS Tools, Whiteout Data, Heaven
  Guardian, etc.) has grown up around the hero-generation/gear-investment
  decision space, suggesting the "which heroes/gear to invest in" choice is
  itself treated as a significant side-activity by the playerbase.
- No traditional "battle pass" was explicitly confirmed by name in the sources
  gathered, though guides describe "multiple concurrent battle-pass-style
  tracks" and premium bundle/VIP shops functioning similarly; one source
  explicitly noted the events guide it consulted "doesn't mention traditional
  battle pass, VIP tiers, or collection systems as separate event mechanics" —
  treat this as an area of incomplete confirmation.

## 8. Early game (first session / first few days)

- Onboarding runs through **Chapter Missions** — a guided tutorial questline
  reported as **10 chapters** (with wiki imagery suggesting content indexed up
  to at least c12), split into **Main Quests** (required, gate further
  progression) and **Side Quests** (optional). The wiki documentation of exact
  per-chapter teaching content was thin in available sources — chapters are
  presented visually rather than described mechanically in the wiki page found.
- The starting fantasy is explicitly framed as **survival, not conquest**:
  manage citizens, keep the Furnace running (their life-support system — "if
  your furnace goes out, your people die," per an independent review), respond
  to villager requests/complaints that affect an approval rating.
- **Chief Orders** (city-wide buffs) are _not_ available immediately — they
  require the Chief's House, unlocked only after reaching Furnace level 6,
  meaning a chunk of the systemic depth is deliberately gated past the very
  first sessions.
- Early state-age map milestones pace alliance-facing content: Tundra territory
  (Day 14), Fertile Land (Day 39), Sunfire Castle SvS battleground (Day 53) —
  meaning the earliest days are single-player/city-focused before the alliance
  PvP layer fully engages.
- By the end of the first week, per a beginner guide, the expectation is the
  base has "shift[ed] from pure survival to becoming a city that can punch back,
  with every decision setting your power curve for the weeks ahead" — i.e.,
  first-week choices are explicitly framed as long-term-consequential, not a
  disposable tutorial phase.
- Reviewer-noted early strength: **generous F2P onboarding** — one review calls
  out "early generosity" for free players before the power gap with paying
  players widens later.

## 9. Mid game

- The loop shifts from tutorial-following to **priority management** across
  several concurrent systems: keep resource production (esp. Coal)
  uninterrupted, keep the Infirmary/hospital resourced to heal injured troops
  after battles (neglecting this "slows down your progress," per a strategy
  guide), and run gathering/exploration teams using a standard
  tank/damage/support hero formation.
- **Alliance choice becomes a major mid-game lever:** guides explicitly
  recommend an active mid-ranked alliance over an inactive high-power one, since
  alliance activity level (not just raw power) drives growth speed and
  protection from stronger players.
- **Research and gear investment decisions compound:** with three research trees
  (Growth/Economy/Battle) and two gear tracks (Hero/Chief) running in parallel,
  mid-game is largely about sequencing these investments against the recurring
  event calendar (Bear Hunt every ~2 days, weekly Fortress Battles, monthly SvS,
  hero generations every ~80 days) rather than discovering new mechanics.
- Mid-game is where the **hero-generation power-creep treadmill** starts to bite
  — community guidance to "focus on one key upgrade per generation and save the
  rest" is explicitly mid/late-game advice, implying players must continuously
  re-triage which heroes/gear to invest in as new generations arrive roughly
  every 80 days.

## 10. Late/end game

- **Structural endgame content:** Furnace progression caps at level 30, after
  which mature servers unlock the **Fire Crystal (FC1-FC10)** era as the "real"
  endgame building progression layer; troop tiers cap around **T11 ("Helios")**,
  with T12 referenced as an endgame/late bracket. The **War Academy** unlocks
  around server-day 230 as a late-game system.
- **What top players actually do:** per the independent deep-dive review,
  endgame is dominated by the **State vs State (SvS)** monthly cycle, **Fortress
  Battles** weekly territory contests, and continual **hero-generation
  gear/investment chasing** — i.e., the late game is a recurring PvP-and-event
  calendar rather than a linear content wall to "beat."
- **Where it becomes a grind/whale treadmill:** the same review is explicit that
  the power gap between free and paying players becomes "extreme" at endgame via
  VIP tiers, hero shard purchases, and gear tiers like "T4 Legendary Gear" —
  concluding that "**a single Whale who has spent USD 50,000 can literally wipe
  out an entire alliance of F2P players**." A widely-discussed Hacker
  News-linked blog post documents one player who spent **over $31,000** on the
  game, describing the monetization as "a game of progressively longer timers,
  resource crunches, and anything to make you feel stuck so you will spend" — HN
  commenters characterized this as effectively "unregulated black-box gambling"
  aimed at capturing "whales," a term commenters noted the industry itself
  associates with players who have addiction issues.
- **Content-runs-out signal:** the recurring generational power-creep cycle (new
  heroes ~every 80 days) functions as the game's primary "new content" cadence
  at endgame, rather than new narrative or geographic content — meaning
  veteran/top-spending players are effectively on a permanent gear-and-hero
  replacement treadmill rather than reaching a stable "mastered" state.

## 11. Community sentiment

Note: direct fetching of reddit.com (including old.reddit.com) was blocked in
this research environment ("Claude Code is unable to fetch from
reddit.com/old.reddit.com/www.reddit.com"), so no live subreddit threads could
be read directly. The sentiment below is drawn from independent long-form
reviews, aggregated App Store/Google Play review summaries, a Hacker News
discussion thread (itself discussing/reacting to a player's personal blog post
about the game), and community guide sites — not primary subreddit quotes. This
is a real gap in the requested coverage; treat the subreddit-specific angle as
unconfirmed.

**Praised:**

- Polished presentation and production values; visually and thematically
  distinct in the SLG genre (the Frostpunk-style frozen-apocalypse setting is
  repeatedly called out as differentiated from typical "medieval kingdom" or
  "zombie" SLG reskins).
- Strong community/social bonds — alliance chat and associated Discord servers
  are described by one reviewer as where "the true 'game' happens," suggesting
  the social layer is a major retention driver independent of the mechanical
  loop.
- Accessible learning curve and stable servers/reliable uptime.
- Constant content cadence via rotating events keeps daily/weekly engagement
  high.
- QoL touches are specifically praised, e.g., the Drill Camp hero-level-sync
  feature ("drastically reducing the grind") and the idle/one-tap hero dispatch
  loop.
- Some App Store reviewers described it positively as an approachable first
  survival-strategy game with "great rewards and on-point daily missions" (per
  aggregated App Store review search).

**Recurring complaints:**

- **Aggressive, escalating monetization** is the single most consistent
  complaint across every source type: App Store/Play review summaries describe a
  "cash grab" pattern, players "spend without spending" incrementally ("Oh
  $4.99 won't hurt" → "$20 here and $30 there"), and reviewers explicitly warn
  that "progress depends almost entirely on how much you pay."
- **Pay-to-win power gap:** reviews and the independent deep-dive both state
  whales can decisively dominate F2P players/alliances at the high end; one
  Google Play review summary claims inability to "catch up to whales no matter
  how hard they grind."
- **Predatory-design concerns at the extreme end:** the $31k-spend blog post
  (via Hacker News) and its comment thread frame the monetization loop as
  functionally gambling-adjacent — "progressively longer timers, resource
  crunches" designed to create a feeling of being stuck, deliberately built to
  extract spend from players "with addiction issues" who effectively subsidize
  the F2P base, with commenters noting mobile games face none of the regulatory
  disclosure requirements (e.g., published odds) that casinos do.
- **Repetitive/tedious resource gathering** flagged by an independent review
  (3.5/5) as compounding into a general staleness over time, alongside minor
  structural gripes like being unable to run two troop-gathering marches
  simultaneously, and unresolvable narrative "villager complaint" flavor text
  that some found irritating rather than immersive.
- **Misleading advertising:** a recurring Google Play complaint theme is that
  the game's marketing ads bear little resemblance to actual gameplay, and that
  the core loop feels like a "copy-paste" of sibling SLG titles (Last War:
  Survival was named as a comparison).
- **Customer support/moderation complaints:** one Google Play review thread
  alleged support was unresponsive to non-paying players and to reports of
  racial/religious abuse in chat, though this is a single-source claim and not
  independently corroborated here.
