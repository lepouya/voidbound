# Tiles Survive! (FunPlus)

## 1. Overview

- **Genre:** 4X-lite mobile strategy / survival city-builder, zombie-apocalypse
  setting. Marketing copy calls it a "strategy survival game."
- **Developer/Publisher:** FunPlus (FunPlus International AG), the studio behind
  **State of Survival**, **Age of Origins**, **Last War: Survival**, and **Sea
  of Conquest**. Coverage repeatedly frames it as FunPlus applying its
  established zombie-SLG playbook to a new tile-exploration hook — one YouTube
  video is titled "Tiles Survive Breakdown: FunPlus goes full Century Games"
  (Century Games makes Whiteout Survival-style rivals), i.e. outside observers
  read this as FunPlus chasing that competitive-SLG template.
- **Release date:** Soft-launched around August 2024; global launch reported
  variously as July 3, 17, 19, or 21, 2025 depending on outlet — treat
  "mid-to-late July 2025" as the safe window. Sensor Tower/AppMagic-sourced
  coverage says the game "started to scale" only from June 2025, i.e. the real
  commercial ramp lagged the soft launch by close to a year.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android; one aggregator also lists a Windows PC client
  (likely emulator-wrapped rather than native, per genre norm).
- **Scale:** FunPlus announced **20 million player downloads** by April 2026 (~9
  months post-global-launch) and **30 million lifetime downloads** at its 1st
  anniversary (~July 2026). AppMagic-sourced estimates put lifetime revenue at
  roughly **$70 million** as of mid-2026. A mobilegamer.biz data
  digest citing Sensor Tower gave a single recent month at ~700K downloads /
  ~$700K revenue on Google Play vs. ~1M downloads / ~$3M revenue on the App
  Store — iOS earns disproportionately more per download, typical for the genre.
- **Monetization:** Free-to-play with IAP, $0.99-$99.99 price points on the App
  Store. Standard SLG stack: VIP levels, gacha-style hero recruitment "banners,"
  Monthly/Weekly Passes, event bundles, speed-up/resource bundles. Third-party
  spending guides (packsify.com) call it explicitly pay-to-win for
  competitive/top-server play, while calling it playable free at a
  casual/alliance-support level.
- **Duplicate/near-duplicate listing:** A second Google Play listing, **"Tiles
  of Survive!"** (`com.googleplay.tos.frontline`, also seen as
  `com.googleplay.vertical.frontline` on APK mirrors), sits alongside the
  primary listing (`com.funplus.ts.global`); a further regional variant "Tiles
  Survive! - VN" (`com.googleplay.ts.vn`) also exists. All three store
  descriptions are near-verbatim. These read as FunPlus's own region/soft-launch
  build variants under different package IDs (a common SLG pattern for
  A/B-testing store pages or staggering regional launches) rather than a
  third-party clone — no source confirms common ownership outright, so treat
  that reading as inferred, not confirmed.

## 2. Core gameplay loop

A session: check production/timers → clear a tile of fog to expand territory →
assign/rebalance survivor job slots → spend resources on building upgrades or
hero leveling → run one or more PvE raids for resources/hero materials → check
alliance help-requests/rally opportunities → collect event progress.

- **Tile-by-tile fog clearing** is the signature hook: each revealed tile can
  hold a new biome, a zombie threat, a resource node, ruins, blueprints, or loot
  ("souvenirs"). Clearing is gated by Power Plant capacity — captured tiles
  raise electricity demand, so expansion has to stay paced behind power
  infrastructure or buildings stop functioning.
- **Survivor job assignment**: survivors take roles (hunter, chef, lumberjack,
  etc.) whose output is modulated by health, morale, and food/rest — an ongoing
  layer distinct from hero/combat.
- **"Wire electricity" mechanic**: production buildings need power to run at
  full speed; guides describe the Power Plant as the central progression gate
  ("no building can exceed Power Plant capacity"), recommending it stay level-
  matched or ahead of the Settlement.
- **Hero recruitment and leveling** runs in parallel via gacha banners.
- **PvE raid loops** (Burrow Conquest, Doomsday Express, Dire Dispatch — §5) are
  repeated for resources, hero-upgrade materials (Behemoth Cells), and XP.

## 3. Base building & progression systems

- The base is the **Settlement** (one third-party guide, packsify.com, calls it
  "Sanctuary" instead — possibly an older/localized name; official FunPlus copy
  consistently says "Settlement").
- **Building priority** per guides: economy first (Farm + Lumberyard in
  parallel), Steel Plant around Settlement level 12-15 once steel becomes the
  bottleneck, Power Plant kept level-matched or ahead as a hard gate, Storage
  sized to hold ≥12 hours of offline production.
- **Heroes**: gacha-recruited, tiered **SR**/**SSR**. Named heroes include Sarge
  (SR), Rosie, Freja (SR), Layla/Leila, Tarzan, Ray, Maddie, Travis (SR), Tara,
  Chef (SR), Ghost, Eva, Lucky, Rusty, Nikola. Pocket Gamer's tier list (Aug
  2026): **S** — Rosie, Freja, Layla, Tarzan; **A** — Sarge, Ray, Maddie,
  Travis, Tara; **B** — Chef, Ghost, Eva, Lucky; **C** — everyone else. Heroes
  fill combat roles (tank/DPS/support) and double as
  resource-gathering/exploration units. Tara is called a "keystone" hero that
  unlocks a specific formation ("4 Guardians + 1 Gunner") — synergy/unlock
  gating matters as much as raw power in places.
- **Chief Gear**: account-wide gear progression (quality tiers white →
  legendary), compounding across the whole roster rather than per-hero.
- **Behemoths**: a fifth-team-member mechanic (e.g. "Gryphon") acting through
  cooldown reduction on hero ultimates rather than flat stats — guides claim a
  well-fed Behemoth's cooldown reduction can outweigh a 10-15% raw power
  deficit.
- **Research/tech tree**: Development, Economy, and Heroes trees unlock first;
  later, Intel Post and Doomsday Express trees; a mid-2026 patch added a "Might
  of the Ocean" tree (Settlement Techs) granting Power bonuses to "Mariner"
  heroes. Guides recommend Economy research before Military. A Laboratory
  building governs research speed/tiers alongside the Power Plant.
- **Power/CP score**: "Power" is the account-wide strength score guides optimize
  for, driven by Power Plant level, research, troop tier/count, hero level/gear,
  and Behemoths — the genre-standard single number for matchmaking/leaderboards.

## 4. Resource economy

- **Base production resources:** Food, Wood/Lumber, Steel (steel becomes the
  binding constraint from roughly Settlement level 12-15 on).
- **Premium currency:** "Gems" (per Pocket Gamer's redemption-code rewards). A
  separate guide (packsify.com) names a purchase-only "Waypoints" currency for
  Passes — unclear if this is a synonym for Gems or a distinct currency; flagged
  as an inconsistency across sources rather than resolved.
- **Progression-gating materials:** Adhesives (gate Chief Gear upgrades, mainly
  from the alliance boss Ghoulion Pursuit, distributed by personal damage
  bracket); Behemoth Cells (from Doomsday Express, level Behemoths); hero
  fragments; gear/reforge materials; Signature Weapon materials.
- **Spend sinks:** building upgrades, troop training, research, hero
  leveling/star-advancement (gacha pulls), gear forging/reforging,
  event-specific currencies (e.g. Festive Pinball tokens, §7), Behemoth feeding.
- **Storage/caps:** guides frame storage in terms of hours of offline production
  banked (≥12h recommended) rather than a hard numeric cap. No source explicitly
  described raid-looting of another player's stored resources for this game
  specifically.

## 5. Combat systems

**PvE:**

- **Burrow Conquest** — territory-expansion mode; begins with fog clearance,
  captured tiles raise electricity demand (tying back to the Power Plant gate).
  Late-game play shifts from solo tile-clearing to alliance-coordinated timing
  and reinforcement.
- **Doomsday Express** — repeatable raid with refreshable targets, run for
  Behemoth Cells and XP.
- **Dire Dispatch** — another PvE raid/dispatch system; patch 2.4.7 (mid-2026)
  added "Mega Modes" to both it and Doomsday Express, automating what had been
  manual per-raid grinding.
- **Biomes:** coverage names six, from early-game "Overgrown Forest" to
  late-game "Frozen Wasteland" (weather hazards), plus a dedicated "Infected
  Zone" biome for zombie-combat encounters.

**PvP / alliance warfare:**

- **Arena** and **Cross-Server** competition for individual ranking.
- **Rallies** — alliance members combine strength against neutral targets or
  rival bases; one rally can combine up to 5 allied teams.
- **Garrison/Tower mechanics** — garrisoned troops can't heal, so alliances time
  reinforcements so a fresh march slides in the instant a damaged one withdraws.
  Holding a Tower drains 2% of its troops per attack, and the same alliance
  holding it longer compresses the attack cadence from every 4 minutes down to
  every 1 minute.
- **Kill-event scoring**: three streams — troop losses (base points, scaled by
  unit level), enemy kills (higher points, also level-scaled), and occupation
  (10,000 points per 60 seconds holding Arcadia or a tower).
- **"Ghost Zap"** — a community-documented tactic: send a cheap, fast unit to
  flip an undefended tower, triggering an instant 2% health "zap" against the
  Arcadia target the moment it flips, then abandon and repeat. Cited as an
  example of coordinated play beating raw power.
- **Recurring competitive events**: Oil Clash (map/territory control), Turbulent
  Tides (resource accumulation), Arcadian Conquest (alliance scoring), Turbo
  Turtle and Power Play (weekly anchors), Reservoir Raid and Ghoulion Pursuit
  rotations (seasonal). Season III was reportedly "Copper Clash," adding
  faction-based competition.

## 6. Alliance/social systems

- Joining an alliance "as early as possible" is standard guide advice —
  alliances give construction/research speed assistance and building help that
  "applies retroactively," plus rally participation, shop rewards, and event
  access.
- The **Ghoulion Pursuit** alliance boss gates Adhesives by personal
  damage-dealt bracket, creating a compounding loop: better gear → more damage →
  better Adhesive tier → faster next-tier gear.
- **Server/State identity**: guide language ("states," "some states are way
  stronger than others") indicates the standard SLG server/state model; one App
  Store review complains state-power imbalance "make[s] challenges completely
  pointless" for weaker states.
- **State transfers**: mentioned as available to more developed servers, gated
  by power thresholds and limited windows — standard SLG end-game mobility.
- No source surfaced specifics on alliance chat, cross-alliance diplomacy, or
  gifting beyond a general note that "social gifting mechanics encourage
  non-paying users to explore premium options" (marlvel.ai) and that Season
  Weekly Passes are giftable. This section is thinner than others — treat
  alliance social tooling as under-documented, not confirmed absent.

## 7. Additional/side features

(Kept separate from the core loop, per the template — events, mini-games, and
collection systems layered on top.)

- **Battle Pass / Season Pass**: paid tier unlocks rewards at every milestone
  during active seasons; Season Weekly Passes are giftable to friends.
- **1st Anniversary event** (~July 2026): "State-wide cooperation, limited
  mini-games, community rewards, decorations, double-drop activities and several
  progression tracks."
- **Festive Pinball**: a standalone mini-game with its own event token; climbs a
  points leaderboard, 5,000 points unlocks a permanent event-exclusive base
  skin.
- **Fishing Tournament / Bulk Fishing Mode**: consume multiple event items in
  one attempt for multiplied points.
- **Gift/redemption codes**: FunPlus distributes periodic codes (live August
  2026 examples: CAT0808, HUGDAY2026, TS777, TS888, TS999) via Facebook and
  Discord, redeemable for Gems, items, or Energy. Pocket Gamer maintains an
  actively updated codes page and hero tier list.
- **Collectibles/decoration**: "souvenirs" from tile exploration, plus
  base-skin/decoration unlocks (e.g. the Festive Pinball skin above).

## 8. Early game (first session / first few days)

- **Sarge** narrates the setup (zombie outbreak, building a stronghold) and
  walks the player through "powering up defenses, organizing survivors, and
  taking the first steps in building a shelter."
- Structured as **chapters**, each with a concrete objective (upgrade a
  building, rescue a survivor, gather a resource); completing one unlocks the
  next map/biome.
- Early buildings: Power Plant, Houses, Kitchens; early roles: hunter, chef,
  lumberjack.
- Reportedly includes **branching narrative/story decision points** early on
  that "teach consequences of choice-making" (BlueStacks) — lighter-weight than
  most SLGs bother with at tutorial stage, though no source details how
  mechanically consequential (vs. cosmetic) the branching actually is.
- Guides warn against expanding tile territory faster than Power Plant capacity
  supports, framing early pacing as "deliberate, measured progression."
- Packsify calls the first ~30 days "the highest-return investment window your
  Tiles Survive account will ever have" — early settlement layout, hero-core
  selection, and alliance placement compound for months, so guides push new
  players to get these decisions right early rather than optimize later.

## 9. Mid game

- Once the guided chapters taper off (roughly "Month 2+" per packsify), the loop
  shifts toward **Chief Gear** progression as Adhesive income stabilizes, and
  **Behemoth** development as a competitive differentiator between
  similarly-geared accounts.
- Research priority shifts from Economy-first to layering in Military/combat
  trees once resource generation stops being the bottleneck.
- PvE raids (Burrow Conquest, Doomsday Express, Dire Dispatch) become the
  steady-state material treadmill, later partially automated by "Mega Modes"
  (patch 2.4.7) to reduce manual-tap fatigue.
- Alliance participation becomes load-bearing: Rallies, Garrison/Tower contests,
  and Ghoulion Pursuit are the main vehicles for the Adhesive treadmill from
  here on.

## 10. Late/end game

- Top-tier play is explicitly gated by spend tier per packsify's breakdown:
  ~$500/month "Competitive Floor" to be relevant in serious alliance play,
  $1,000-$3,000/month "Competitive Core" for top-tier accounts, and
  $3,000-$10,000+/month "Server Dominance" for top server positioning and
  alliance leadership.
- Free-to-play accounts are explicitly told their ceiling is **alliance
  participation, not solo ranking** — the guide states F2P play "cannot match a
  competitive spender's individual account power."
- Recurring seasonal events (Oil Clash, Arcadian Conquest, Ghoulion Pursuit
  rotations, "Copper Clash" faction seasons) are the ongoing end-game content
  cadence; guides recommend planning 30-60 days ahead of these windows.
- One review aggregator (marlvel.ai) states the game "lacks real-time tactical
  combat and deeper end-game mechanics found in competitors like Last Shelter:
  Survival, limiting long-term engagement depth" — a single outside opinion, not
  a broad confirmed consensus, but worth noting as a named weak point.
- No source described a defined "campaign completion" or end state — consistent
  with the genre, this reads as an open-ended seasonal/server treadmill with no
  designed finish line.

## 11. Community sentiment

**No active dedicated subreddit was found.** Repeated targeted searches
(`site:reddit.com`, `reddit.com/r/TilesSurvive`, quoted-title searches) turned
up no game-specific subreddit or notable threads — either the community hasn't
coalesced there, or it's too small to surface in search. Flagging this as a real
coverage gap rather than glossing over it.

**App Store (iOS)** — 4.4-4.6★ across roughly 268K-290K ratings depending on
source/date. Quotes pulled directly from the reviews page:

- Positive: _"I'm not one to play games but I decided to download on a whim. I
  am still playing."_ / _"It's been so much fun to connect with people. This
  coming from a committed introvert."_ / _"I have spent lots of hours on this
  game i have tried a lot of games and find myself deleting others."_ / _"If you
  have enjoyed games like WoW or ESO, you will enjoy this."_
- Mixed/negative: _"AFTER the update i now freezes and overheats phones everyone
  i play with have been experiencing issues."_ / _"Some states are way stronger
  than others which make challenges completely pointless."_ / _"More things
  should be easier to achieve without spending money. Not everyone can afford to
  spend."_
- A separate review-mining pass surfaced further complaints: the chat/word
  filter is over-aggressive (a two-month daily player said neutral words like
  "die" get silently filtered); one reviewer said the game **"feels very much
  like it was designed by a man,"** citing overtly-sexualized female character
  art and stereotyped write-ups versus male characters; and a complaint that
  past a certain progression point "it makes you pay for certain things to move
  on," with notification prompts described as "overwhelming."

**Google Play (Android)** — 4.1-4.33★ across roughly 249K-284K ratings depending
on source/date (lower than iOS, consistent with the revenue skew in §1).
Recurring negative themes: heavy IAP reliance, a recent update that **removed
rewards from raids** (read by reviewers as a stealth nerf), **misleading
advertising** (ads show combat/action not representative of the actual
slow-paced building-and-waiting gameplay), crashes, missing features, billing
issues, and update regressions. One review: _"you spend resources way too
quickly and it is heavily pay to win."_ Some reviewers flagged the art as
**AI-generated or AI-adjacent** and the English voiceover as poor ("terrible
English AI voice overs"). Several sources call the game derivative — one
reviewer said it uses **the same opening/story template as Last War, Whiteout
Survival, and Dark War Survival**. Counterpoint from the same pool: some
reviewers call the core loop "fine," the gacha "not greedy," and note no ads
within core play.

A caveat surfaced independently in two searches: aggregator sites (unstar.app,
general review-mining summaries) flagged that **many 5-star Google Play reviews
look like bot or incentivized one-liners** ("Very fun!", "Nice graphics!") —
worth discounting the raw star average somewhat toward the more detailed,
apparently-organic critical reviews.

**"Deceptive advertising" is the single most consistently repeated complaint**
across App Store reviews, Google Play reviews, and third-party review sites
alike — trailers/ads allegedly show action-combat gameplay unrepresentative of
the actual building-and-waiting loop. Notable enough that at least one YouTube
video is dedicated to an "ads vs. gameplay" comparison ("Tiles Survive! - ads vs
gameplay | Badvertising"), though its content couldn't be retrieved — YouTube
pages did not yield fetchable transcript/description text via WebFetch in this
pass, only static nav/footer.

**Guide-content ecosystem**: an active YouTube/text-guide ecosystem exists (full
gameplay playlists, a dedicated Burrow Conquest how-to, a "technology research
complete reveal" video, and guide sites packsify.com, ldshop.gg, lootbar.gg,
buffhub.com, mone.gg, clashiverse.com publishing spending guides, tier lists,
and event breakdowns) — a real, engaged player base oriented around
optimization/spend-efficiency content rather than casual discussion, even
without a Reddit hub.

**Comparative framing**: outside coverage repeatedly places Tiles Survive! in
the same bracket as FunPlus's own **State of Survival** (Pocket Gamer calls it
FunPlus's "most iconic title," also zombie-themed) and as a rival to **Whiteout
Survival**, **Last War: Survival**, and **Last Shelter: Survival** — press and
guide sites treat it as a new entrant in an already-crowded zombie-SLG field,
with tile-exploration/fog-clearing repeatedly cited as the one real point of
differentiation.
