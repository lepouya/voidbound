# State of Survival (FunPlus)

## 1. Overview

- **Genre:** SLG / mobile 4X-lite base-building game with a zombie-apocalypse
  theme; blends real-time base building, hero collection, a single-player
  campaign ("Explorer Trail"), and alliance PvP.
- **Developer/publisher:** FunPlus, via its internal studio KingsGroup (Android
  package `com.kingsgroup.sos`). FunPlus was founded in 2011 (originally
  "Halfquest," Silicon Valley; rebranded 2012 by Andy Zhong and Yitao Guan), is
  headquartered in Switzerland (global HQ opened Dec. 2020) with studios in
  China, Singapore, Portugal, and Spain, and has ~2,000 employees. Other FunPlus
  titles include King of Avalon, Guns of Glory, Sea of Conquest, and Stormshot —
  same base-building/SLG family.
- **Release date:** August 2019 (global mobile). A standalone PC/Steam client
  followed later (Steam app ID 2485460); the App Store now also lists a separate
  "State of Survival: Front War" app alongside the original "Zombie War"
  listing.
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android, Steam/Windows, plus FunPlus's own PC client.
- **Scale** (Udonis/Sensor-Tower-derived stats, as of 2026):
  - All-time downloads ~137.1M (100M by Sept. 2021, 150M by its 3-year
    anniversary in May 2023); all-time revenue ~$1.69B.
  - Peaks: 7.1M monthly downloads (mid-2020), ~$51M monthly revenue (early
    2021), 16.3M MAU (2020).
  - Current (2026): downloads/revenue/MAU all down 91-95% from peak, but still
    ~1.4-1.6M MAU, ~200-300K DAU (13.9% DAU/MAU), and several million dollars of
    monthly revenue six-plus years post-launch. Revenue per download
    ~$12.35, revenue per MAU ~$337/yr, ARPDAU ~$0.79 — a small,
    heavily-monetized, long-tenured base rather than a broad casual one.
  - Android outnumbers iOS ~2.6:1. Top markets US, Western Europe, East Asia;
    skews male, 25-44 the largest segment.
  - By 2024-2025, Sensor Tower's "State of Mobile Gaming" reporting has State of
    Survival eclipsed within its own genre: Last War: Survival and Whiteout
    Survival were the #1/#2 grossing mobile games globally in 2025, a tier State
    of Survival no longer occupies even though it's still commercially viable.
  - Awards: Best Simulation Game, Samsung Best of Galaxy Store (2020); Best
    Strategy Game, Huawei AppGallery Editor's Choice (2021).
- **Monetization:** F2P with heavy IAP, $0.99-$49.99 (Biocaps premium currency
  $0.99-$19.99; Crate/loot-box bundles $2.99-$49.99 in
  Special/Standard/Popular/Giant/Mega sizes; smaller bundles like a $4.99 "Big
  Deal"). Community reviews describe some premium heroes costing "upwards of
  $3,000" to fully upgrade via gacha fragment pulls, and cite expectations of
  $1,000+/month to stay competitive at a high level. A 12-level VIP points
  system with its own discounted store layers on top.

## 2. Core gameplay loop

Spend accumulated resources to advance production, which lets you spend more on
the next tier of upgrade — repeated many times a session, across months. Each
session, a player: (1) collects offline production and clears completed timers
(building/research/troop-training/hero-march); (2) sends idle troops to gather
resources or keeps a PvP march moving; (3) queues the next building upgrade,
research item, or troop batch, always capped by Headquarters (HQ) level — "none
of your buildings can ever be of a higher level than your HQ"; (4) checks
daily/rotating events and spends stamina on "Intel" missions before it caps out
(a full bar stops regenerating); (5) runs a few Explorer Trail (single-player)
stages with a 3-hero squad when new content is available; (6) works the alliance
— mutual speed-up help, alliance-wide events (Trap, Championship), and Lookout
Tower threat checks.

Timers are the structural backbone: early sessions are short/frequent
(minutes-long timers), late-game sessions revolve around long multi-hour or
multi-day timers punctuated by short bursts of active play.

## 3. Base building & progression systems

- **HQ as the hard gate** — no other building, hero, or research track can
  exceed current HQ level, making HQ level the primary early/mid-game
  progression currency.
- **Production buildings** (Farm, Lumberyard, Furnace, gas facility) generate
  the four core resources; **military buildings** (Barracks/Infantry,
  Range/Hunters, Garage/Riders) train troops; a **Hospital** heals wounded
  troops rather than losing them outright.
- **Warehouse** caps how much of each resource is protected from looting on
  breach, and gates Trading Post listing volume.
- **Lookout Tower** gives early warning of incoming march size, ETA, and whether
  it's a solo attack or rally.
- **Workshop** (HQ 16) crafts/enhances Hero Gear (three pieces per hero —
  head/body/feet — five tiers Common→Legendary) and, from HQ 22, Chief Gear (16
  six-piece sets buffing troop attack/defense directly).
- **Research Lab / tech tree**, HQ-gated economy/military/defense branches; the
  "Institute of Plasma" is the late-game continuation, spending a separate
  "Plasma" resource.
- **Hero system:** heroes are recruited mainly via gacha-style fragments
  (General Hero Fragments are a wildcard currency; specific fragments come from
  events, hero-search "Lucky Draw," and VIP passes). Heroes belong to one of
  three combat classes (Infantry/Hunter/Rider), lead troop formations, give
  passive bonuses, and have unique active "Explorer Skills" for the campaign.
  Hero rarity/gear tier are major Battle Power/CP inputs.
- **Chief (player avatar) progression:** its own talent tree and Chief Gear,
  separate from heroes, guides describe as having "an outstanding impact on
  progression until the late game."
- **Power/CP score:** an aggregate Battle Power number (HQ/building levels,
  troop count/tier, hero rarity/level/gear, Chief talents/gear, tech) used as
  the at-a-glance strength comparison between players/alliances/states — the
  number whales visibly dwarf free players on.

## 4. Resource economy

- **Core resources:** Food, Wood, Metal, Gas — used across construction, troop
  training, and research.
- **Production:** passive from owned buildings, active via troops dispatched to
  map resource tiles (nine node tiers, higher yielding more per trip).
- **Storage/protection:** Warehouse caps how much of each resource survives a
  breach; the deliberate design leaves surplus above that cap exposed, creating
  "spend it or risk losing it" pressure against hoarding.
- **Spend sinks:** building upgrades, troop training, research, hero-gear
  crafting/enhancement, and (per some accounts) buying resources outright with
  Biocaps to skip a shortfall.
- **Alternative acquisition:** PvP raiding/looting unprotected surplus, the
  Trading Post (Warehouse-gated exchange), and alliance gifting/help.
- **Secondary late-game resource:** "Plasma," feeding the Institute of Plasma
  tech tree.
- **Premium currency:** Biocaps (purchasable, small free trickle) buy speed-ups,
  Crates, VIP Store goods, and direct resource/booster packs.

## 5. Combat systems

**PvE:** the **Explorer Trail** single-player campaign sends a 3-hero, troopless
squad through numbered chapters of zombie encounters, unlocking story beats and
rewards — the primary vehicle for narrative content and steady hero-fragment
income. **Doomsday** events add tougher zombie variants (including a "Titan"
type). **Daily Intel** missions (three refreshes/day, stamina-gated) reward hero
fragments, troops, combat manuals, skill books, and badges — guides call these
better value than regular quests, pushing players to keep stamina spent rather
than capped. **Alliance Trap** is a rotating alliance-wide PvE target, rewards
by individual damage ranking.

**PvP:** **world-map raiding** hits other players' bases/marches directly for
loot; **rallies** combine alliance members' troops against a fortified target.
**State vs. State (SvS/"State Warfare")** is the marquee large-scale event, run
in three phases:

1. **Preparatory** (multi-day) — point-scoring; the winner gets a 15% healing
   buff and the right to attack the loser's capital.
2. **War/Capital Clash** (~26 hours) — up to three teleports into enemy
   territory, contesting four Fortified Facilities and towers, then the Capital
   (triple points); attackers can't hit a defender's HQ more than 3 levels below
   their own. Victory needs 6-8+ consecutive hours holding the capital, or the
   longest cumulative occupation.
3. **Recovery** (~36 hours) — healing-speed bonuses and troop revival (base 30%,
   resurrection kits +10%, alliance assist +50%, capped ~90%) so a losing state
   isn't crippled.

Winning both phases lets the winning alliance appoint a Governor over the losing
state; losing both leaves a state governor-less for two weeks. **Garrisons:**
troops stationed in Alliance Towers can only be wounded (sent to Hospital), not
killed, though troops at your own HQ remain fully exposed. **Arena** is a
smaller ranked PvP ladder for free currency rewards.

## 6. Alliance/social systems

- **Structure:** rank-based leadership (R4/R5-style officers) handling
  recruitment, events, territory, and diplomacy.
- **Alliance Buildings/Towers:** shared, garrison-able structures that anchor
  territory control during SvS.
- **Mutual aid:** members speed up each other's queues; alliance membership is
  repeatedly cited (wiki, guides, F2P tip sheets) as the single highest-leverage
  free thing a new player can do.
- **Diplomacy:** alliances can negotiate rather than fight — e.g. pooling the
  strongest players from allied alliances to attack/defend a shared Capital.
- **State Merge:** since October 2021, FunPlus periodically merges depopulated
  old states into fewer, denser ones (each "main state" absorbs up to four
  sub-states), with ~1 week's notice; only accounts inactive 15+ days are pruned
  rather than merged. This is the explicit developer mechanism for keeping
  years-old servers from feeling dead as population attrites.
- **Gifting:** alliance-wide reward pools trigger when any member makes a
  purchase, turning individual spend into a shared benefit.

## 7. Additional/side features (separate from the core loop)

- **Licensed-IP crossovers** are the highest-profile growth lever. The Walking
  Dead crossover (March 2021, Daryl Dixon as a playable hero) reportedly brought
  20 million new players — about a fifth of the download base at the time —
  helping push the game past 100M downloads by September 2021. Later crossovers
  include Tomb Raider (Lara Croft hero, HQ skins, march skin, avatar frames, a
  "Path of Growth" event track) and a Warner Bros./Joker crossover, plus
  real-world tie-ins (UFC) and offline anniversary events (e.g. Berlin).
- **Arms Race:** a recurring 28-day event in four 7-day chapters.
- **VIP system:** 12 levels from VIP Points, unlocking a discounted store and
  passive bonuses — a parallel progression track. General Hero Fragments
  (wildcard) and "Lucky Draw" gacha pulls are often bundled into VIP-pass
  purchases.
- **Achievements/Badges (Medals and Titles):** account-wide completionist layer.
- **Task Pass / State Event Task Pass:** battle-pass-style tiered rewards tied
  to periodic state events.
- **Cosmetics:** skins, avatar frames, base decorations, no combat effect.

## 8. Early game (first session / first few days)

Onboarding is a tight, guided main-mission chain (rewarding speed-ups,
resources, hero shards) rather than free exploration; the commonly-cited
benchmark is reaching HQ level 6 within ~8 hours to unlock a second building
queue (parallel construction) — a major pacing unlock. Joining an alliance
immediately is universal advice across every source consulted, since alliance
bonuses compound the earlier they start. Guides also steer new players toward
newer servers (e.g. 800+) over old, established ones, since old servers already
have dominant, deeply-invested alliances a fresh account can't catch up to — an
implicit admission that the PvP layer effectively closes to newcomers over a
server's lifetime. Early timers are minutes-long, encouraging frequent short
check-ins.

## 9. Mid game

Pacing shifts from "unlock the next building" to juggling parallel systems:
resource management across all four types, hero collection/team synergy (guides
cite specific pairings, e.g. Rusty + Chloe), balanced troop composition, and
growing alliance-event participation (Alliance Showdown, Doomsday) for rewards
unavailable solo. Alliance coordination becomes explicitly necessary rather than
optional. Hero gear (Workshop, HQ 16) and its Common→Legendary tier ladder
become a second progression axis alongside building/tech levels.
Stamina/daily-mission discipline (don't let it cap, prioritize Intel) becomes
something the player actively manages — the loop starts rewarding login
consistency over raw session length.

## 10. Late/end game — where it goes after years, and where it wears thin

This is the section with the deepest evidence, given the game's 6+ year history
and large, still-active spender base.

- **SvS/State Warfare is the terminal competitive content** — the multi-phase
  capital siege in §5 is what guides, the wiki, and players describe as the
  actual endgame, not any single-player content. It demands full alliance
  coordination, teleport items, and troop investment far beyond anything the
  campaign asks for.
- **Institute of Plasma and Chief Gear (HQ 22+)** are the late-game power tracks
  once the core building/tech tree is exhausted — growth becomes about gear
  tiers and talent optimization rather than new things to build.
- **Whale-vs-F2P gap widens with tenure, by players' own account.** A long-time
  player (Steam Community, "Waclaw83," Aug 2023, on Realm 356, an early server)
  wrote the game shifted over its life "from 'group up to stop a whale and be
  happy about it' to 'make sure you have enough shields to hide from whales,'"
  that early balance eroded progressively, that old servers are now dominated by
  a small population of high spenders who stay mostly because they're
  financially "trapped" by sunk cost, concluding "don't bother with this one,
  it's a waste of your time." Another poster with 2+ years of F2P play
  ("OG-Rev," same thread family) called it "a huge money pit," noted 1,225+
  states exist with many "dead or dying," that some spenders have reportedly put
  in "tens of thousands" of dollars, and that state-transfer is gated behind a
  ready credit card and months-long waits.
- **A separate Steam thread** (another 2+ year player) put it starkly: "a whale
  can literally 1-shot a f2p player for all they have in terms of troops,"
  naming newer high-power content — advanced heroes, helicopters, planes — as
  having widened rather than narrowed the divide over time, concluding the
  late-life game is "just a money sink for whales and nothing else."
- **State Merge is the structural answer to server death**, not really an
  admitted failure: FunPlus has run recurring merges since October 2021
  specifically to consolidate the depopulated old states the players above
  describe from the inside — old-server decline is treated as an expected,
  managed part of the life cycle rather than an emergency.
- **Business-side confirmation of decline-but-not-death:** downloads/MAU/
  revenue are all down 90%+ from 2020-2021 peaks, yet the game still nets
  several million dollars a month from a shrunken, high-value remaining base
  (revenue-per-download ~$12, high for mass-market mobile strategy) — consistent
  with "the whales who remain are deeply invested and keep paying," and with
  State of Survival having been passed in the charts by newer entrants in its
  own genre (Whiteout Survival, Last War: Survival).
- **What sustains long-tenured non-whales:** the recurring advice is
  alliance-embeddedness (shared rewards, mutual timer help) and event
  participation, not solo grinding — the social/alliance layer, not new solo
  content, is what's cited as the reason people keep logging in years in.
- **Licensed crossovers double as late-game retention**, not just an
  early-funnel tool — Tomb Raider (2024) and Walking Dead reruns keep injecting
  "new-ish" content (a hero, a story arc, cosmetics) into a game whose core tree
  has long since been maxed out by its veteran base.

## 11. Community sentiment

**Sourcing caveat:** direct WebFetch access to reddit.com (all path/subdomain
variants tried, including old.reddit.com and the JSON endpoint) was blocked in
this research environment, so r/StateofSurvival could not be read first-hand,
and web search did not surface enough distinct, quotable reddit.com threads to
substitute. The quotes below come instead from Steam Community discussion
threads (whose tone/content — whale/F2P gap, sunk-cost complaints, "don't
bother" warnings — closely tracks what search snippets indicated the subreddit
contains), Metacritic user reviews, App Store/Google Play review excerpts, and
review/guide sites. Treat this section as a reasonable but incomplete proxy for
the subreddit specifically.

- **Pay-to-win severity is the single most consistent complaint** across every
  source. Metacritic users call it "the worst P2W game there possibly is"; a
  Google Play reviewer wrote "Free Play is a lie... can't keep up or compete
  without spending a lot," citing a $250 special hero base and $50
  per 2 hero fragments, with an expectation of "$1000+ per month to compete at a
  basic level"; a Steam reviewer called it "extremely intelligent in its P2W
  approach to luring millions of players to sink in hundreds of dollars just to
  buy time." Steam's aggregate is "Mixed" (~56% positive of ~450-470 reviews);
  Metacritic's user score is a harsher 3.6/10 (likely skewed by that platform's
  typical review-bombing, but directionally consistent).
- **Grind/wall after the honeymoon period:** several reviews independently
  describe a strong first day or two followed by a hard wall — "the game grinds
  to a halt, as you suddenly need to wait hours for buildings to be upgraded,
  even after using speed-ups... this is where the paywall kicks in."
- **Losing progress to a single stronger player:** Google Play reviews cite
  "months of progress being destroyed in 2 minutes by one player," the sharp end
  of the whale-vs-F2P gap from §10, plus "the devs ignore complaints too."
- **Sunk-cost/stagnant old servers:** as in §10, long-tenured Steam posters
  describe old servers as dominated by a shrinking population of heavy spenders
  who feel "trapped," and advise newcomers away from old servers or the game
  altogether at this point in its life.
- **Praised — breadth/polish of the content package:** Hardcore Droid's review
  says the game "offers a huge amount of content for an Android game and those
  who put in the effort will likely find great value." BlueStacks praises the
  genre-blend itself — base-building conquest, real-time PvP, and a genuine
  single-player campaign (Explorer Trail, a lightweight Sgt. Joe Nolan story) —
  as "an unprecedented" combination "that may appeal to a broader audience,"
  calling the single-player component "a fantastic single player experience" in
  its own right.
- **Praised — F2P viability, with heavy caveats:** F2P guide sites
  (state-of-survival.net, stateofsurvivalguide.com) maintain "you don't need to
  spend money to grow in the game" and that disciplined play (alliance,
  consistent activity, stamina management, events) lets F2P players "reach top
  levels" — in tension with the harsher veteran accounts in §10, which suggest
  F2P viability applies more to personal power growth than to being competitive
  in top-tier PvP (SvS, rallies) against whales.
- **Mixed — the monetization is seen as deliberate craft, not neglect.** Even
  critical reviewers ("extremely intelligent in its P2W approach") tend to frame
  the funnel as well-executed rather than amateurish — a tuned system, not a
  rough edge likely to get fixed.
